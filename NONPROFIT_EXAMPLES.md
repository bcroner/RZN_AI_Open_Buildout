# Nonprofit examples: volunteering in an open buildout

> **Illustrative only.** Sophie's Bakeries, Emma's Threads and Brian's Equine Welfare are
> made-up examples, to show how people could join a nonprofit buildout and what they
> would agree to. They are not real organizations, and no real person is involved.
> This is not legal advice.

A **for-profit** buildout pays every contributor; see the
[Contributor Bill of Rights](README.md#6-contributors-are-paid-either-way). A **nonprofit**
buildout can also welcome **volunteers**, people who give their time freely to a
charitable cause. Volunteering is only for genuine nonprofits: unpaid work for a
for-profit business isn't volunteering, and labor law doesn't allow it.

---

## The Volunteer Bill of Rights

Every nonprofit buildout in these examples promises its volunteers:

1. **It never costs you to help.** No fees, no required purchases, no uniform you have to
   buy.
2. **Come and go freely.** Sign up for what you want and stop any time, with no penalty and
   no explanation needed.
3. **No non-compete and no exclusivity.** Volunteer anywhere else too.
4. **Volunteers never replace a paid job.** If a role would otherwise be someone's
   paycheck, it's a paid role, not a volunteer one.
5. **Safety first.** Training, supervision and safety gear are provided before any task
   that needs them, free of charge.
6. **Expenses are published up front.** Each nonprofit states in advance which expenses it
   reimburses (for example mileage) and at what rate, the same for everyone.
7. **No conditions.** No faith, political view or membership is required to volunteer.
8. **You're told why.** If your offer to help isn't taken up, you get a reason.
9. **Your name is yours.** You're only thanked publicly, or pictured, if you say yes.
10. **Minimal personal data,** used only to organize the volunteering.

## Sophie's Bakeries

**Mission:** A free meal for anyone who walks in: no questions, no forms, no proof of need.
Robot chefs bake fresh bread on the premises, and robot servers bring it to the table. A
location could be a flagship bakery or a storefront in an ordinary shopping district.
Fresh bread also goes out to local food banks, a natural partner for the
[food insecurity work in `philanthropisch`](https://github.com/bcroner/philanthropisch).

**What the robots do, and what people do.** The robots handle the baking and serving.
People do what robots can't: welcoming guests, keeping the room safe and comfortable, and
getting bread to the food banks.

| Volunteer opportunity | Time | What "done" looks like | Who can join |
|---|---|---|---|
| Host: greet and seat guests | 3 hours | Everyone who walked in was welcomed and found a seat, no questions asked | Anyone, after a 30-minute orientation that includes how to pause the robots |
| Dining room helper | 3 hours | Tables cleared and wiped, spills handled, guests who need a hand helped | Anyone, after orientation |
| Food bank delivery run | 2 hours | Bread signed for at each food bank on the route sheet | Valid driver's license and insurance |
| Accessibility check | 1 hour | A written report on anything hard to reach or use, including robot paths | Anyone |
| Recipe testing with the chefs' operators | Flexible | A recipe works at the robots' batch size, with ingredient cost per loaf recorded | Anyone, alongside a trained operator |

**Paid, not volunteer:** robot technicians and the kitchen operators who program and
supervise the robots. Those roles keep the kitchen safe, and they're real jobs, so they're
posted as paid work.

**Gated for safety:**
- The kitchen is a **licensed commercial kitchen** under local health-department rules,
  whether the baking is done by robots or people. The robots and their food-contact parts
  must meet food-equipment sanitation standards.
- Robots working near guests need **safety guarding, emergency stops, and clear paths**, so
  every volunteer learns how to stop them before their first shift.
- Food bank deliveries follow the food bank's own handling rules for temperature and
  labeling, including allergens.

## Emma's Threads

**Mission:** Clothes that fit and last, free for people who need them: a clothing
closet, free repairs, and sewing classes.

| Volunteer opportunity | Time | What "done" looks like | Who can join |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sort and size donations | 2 hours | Items sorted by size and condition; anything stained or broken set aside | Anyone |
| Repair clinic | 3 hours | Items mended and logged back into stock | Anyone who sews, from hemming up |
| Teach a beginner sewing class | 90 minutes | Every student finishes the class project | Experienced sewists |
| Closet hosting and fittings | 3 hours | Visitors helped to find what fits, with dignity: no questions about why they need it | Anyone, after orientation |
| Pattern and tutorial writing | Flexible | A beginner can follow it without help | Anyone |

**Gated for safety:** anyone working with children needs a **background check**, which the
nonprofit pays for. Children's clothing is checked against **product recall lists** and
safety rules, such as those on drawstrings, before it's given out.

## Brian's Equine Welfare

**Mission:** Rescue, rehabilitate and rehome horses, and give lifelong sanctuary to those
who can't be rehomed.

| Volunteer opportunity | Time | What "done" looks like | Who can join |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barn chores | 3 hours | Stalls cleaned, water and hay checked, feed log signed | Anyone who has completed **safety orientation** |
| Grooming and handling | 2 hours | Horses groomed, with notes on anything unusual reported to staff | After orientation plus a handling assessment |
| Fence and facility repair | A workday | Repairs done to the facility checklist and inspected | Anyone; power tools only for those checked out on them |
| Fundraising and events | Varies | Event run and donations logged | Anyone |
| Adoption follow-up visits | 2 hours | Visit report completed | Trained volunteers |

**Gated for safety:**
- **Veterinary care is for licensed veterinarians only.**
- Hauling horses needs trained drivers with suitable trailers.
- Horses are large, and even calm ones can injure people. So orientation is required,
  closed-toe boots are mandatory, and minors volunteer only with a guardian present.

## How nonprofit and for-profit buildouts connect

- **The People's Share can fund them.** Under the proposal's §5, a buildout with no
  contributors after four quarters sends its held share to `philanthropisch`, which could
  in turn support nonprofits like these.
- **Paid roles still exist.** A nonprofit can post paid work through Open Buildouts too,
  such as a head baker or a barn manager, under the regular Contributor Bill of Rights.
- **Selling goods needs care.** If a nonprofit sells anything, for example bread sold at a
  market to fund the free bread, volunteer rules can change. The labor rules for
  volunteers at a nonprofit's money-making activities are narrower. Check with a lawyer
  before volunteers work on anything sold.

## Before any of these runs for real

- **Nonprofit status:** tax-exempt status, or a fiscal sponsor, so donations are
  tax-deductible and volunteering is clearly charitable.
- **Insurance:** general liability and volunteer accident coverage, especially for the
  kitchen and the barn.
- **A lawyer's review:** volunteer rules, waivers, and background-check requirements where
  the nonprofit operates.
