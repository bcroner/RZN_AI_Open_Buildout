# Example: an adults-only game on the open buildout

> **Illustrative only.** *Tey Voy A Humpar* is a hypothetical game, used to show how the
> open buildout and the 50 / 40 / 10 split work for an adults-only product. This page
> describes the business, not the content. It is not legal advice.

**The idea.** Disturbing Software builds an adults-only massively multiplayer game built
around competitive dance battles, the "twerk-off": players dance to eliminate each other
from the competition, in a world with adult themes for verified adults only.

---

## How the split applies

The game is the Builder's product, so the proposal's §3 split applies to Net Receipts from
any part of it built on RZN AI technology. For example, if AI-driven characters in the game
run on the RZN AI AGI model, $10,000 in Net Receipts in a quarter from that game becomes:

| Share | Amount |
|---|---|
| Disturbing Software (the Builder) | **$5,000** |
| RZN AI | **$4,000** |
| People's Share, for the game's accepted contributors in proportion to what they were paid (§5) | **$1,000** |

If a relied-on patent application ends without a patent, the split becomes 55 / 35 / 10 for
work relying only on it (§8).

### Pricing

Disturbing Software's minimum prices:

| Product | Minimum price |
|---|---|
| MMO subscription, such as *Tey Voy A Humpar* | **$4.99 a month** |

**What a month of subscribers produces.** Say 1,000 players subscribe at $4.99, and
payment processing costs an assumed $0.50 per subscription. Adult-merchant processing
often costs more than ordinary processing, so check real rates. Net Receipts are
$4,490, split as:

| Share | Per month |
|---|---|
| Disturbing Software | $2,245 |

The split applies only to receipts from parts of the game built on RZN AI technology (§3).

## Where people fit in

Adult content changes one thing above all: **nobody should ever be surprised into working
on it.** Every opportunity is labeled, and plenty of the work involves no adult content at
all.

| You are… | What you do | How you join | Adult content? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engine and server contributor** | Networking, matchmaking, servers, anti-cheat, tools | Open opt-in, paid per accepted contribution | **None.** Labeled so |
| **Dance and animation contributor** | Dance moves, rhythm mechanics, music sync, costumes | Open opt-in, paid per accepted contribution | Labeled per opportunity |
| **Adult content artist** | Adult-themed art and animation of fictional adult characters | Open opt-in **only after verifying you're 18 or older** | **Yes.** Clearly labeled |
| **Performer** (voice or motion capture for adult scenes) | Performs as a real person | A negotiated contract, **not** open opt-in | **Yes** |
| **Trust and safety** | Moderates player content and handles reports | Paid staff, **not** open opt-in | Yes, with wellbeing support |

The Contributor Bill of Rights applies to every open opportunity: paid at the posted rate,
a decision with a reason inside the review window, free to leave, no non-compete. And one
more promise for this buildout: **working on the game never requires working on its adult
content.**

## The rules that keep it legal and saleable

These aren't optional. Breaking any one of them can end the business, and some are crimes.

1. **Adults only, everywhere.**
   - **Players:** verified 18 or older. A growing number of U.S. states legally require
     age verification for adult content.
   - **Contributors to adult content:** verified 18 or older.
   - **Every character in adult content** is unmistakably an adult, in appearance, voice,
     and story. Nothing that depicts or suggests a minor, ever.
2. **Sexual content is never combined with violence, death, or lack of consent.** In the
   game, "to death" means elimination from the dance battle, and it stays entirely
   separate from any adult content. Payment processors and platforms generally prohibit
   the combination, and some of it is illegal.
3. **Real people.**
   - Performers are verified adults with signed consent and releases, and record-keeping
     laws for real performers, such as 18 U.S.C. § 2257, may apply.
   - No real person's likeness is used without their written consent, including in
     AI-generated content.
4. **Players' own content.** In an online game, players will upload and say things.
   - **Trust and safety is a paid team,** with reporting tools and wellbeing support.
   - **Child sexual abuse material is zero tolerance:** removed immediately, the account
     banned, and a report made to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children
     as U.S. law requires.
5. **Where it can be sold.**
   - Major console and mobile app stores don't allow adults-only games.
   - Some PC storefronts do, with restrictions.
   - Taking payments needs a payment processor that accepts adult merchants.
   - Plan distribution before building.
